1. Prepare Before You Start Searching

Many freshers start applying randomly without preparation. This is the biggest mistake you can make. Before you send out a single application, spend 3-5 days getting ready:

1

Decide Your Target Roles

Pick 2-3 job roles that match your education and interests. Applying to 50 different roles randomly is far less effective than applying to 20 well-matched roles. For example, a B.Com graduate could target accounts executive, sales executive, or insurance roles — not software development.

2

Create a Strong Resume

Your resume is your first impression. Keep it to one page, use a clean format, and include: a short professional summary, education, skills, projects, internships, and achievements. Quantify everything — "Increased social media reach by 40%" is stronger than "Managed social media." Need help? Read our guide on the top skills every fresher should learn.

3

Optimise Your LinkedIn Profile

Employers and recruiters check LinkedIn before interviews. Use a professional photo, write a clear headline (e.g., "B.Com Graduate | Aspiring Sales Executive"), add your education and skills, and connect with 20-30 people from companies you are interested in.

4

Build Basic Skills

If you have time before starting applications, strengthen your computer skills (MS Excel, MS Word), communication skills, and any role-specific basics. Even 2 weeks of focused practice makes a visible difference in interviews.

Fresher Advantage

Do not let "no experience" discourage you. Companies like BTW Group hire freshers because they are adaptable, energetic, and trainable. Focus your resume and interviews on your skills, projects, and enthusiasm — not on what you lack.

2. Where to Find Fresher Jobs

Knowing where to look is half the battle. Here are the best channels for freshers in 2026:

Job Portals

Naukri, Indeed, LinkedIn Jobs, and Freshersworld are the top platforms. Create a complete profile, upload your resume, set job alerts for your target roles, and apply daily.

Company Career Pages

Many companies post openings only on their own websites first. Follow your target companies' career pages and check them weekly. BTW Group posts all openings on careers.btwgroup.co.

Campus Placements

If you are still in college, campus placement drives are your fastest route to a job. Stay in touch with your TPO (Training & Placement Officer) and register for every drive. Colleges can also partner with companies — learn how.

Referrals & Networking

Tell friends, family, alumni, and neighbours that you are job hunting. Referrals are one of the most effective ways to get shortlisted. Also attend job fairs and industry events in your city.

Internships First

If full-time jobs are scarce, start with an internship. Many internships convert into full-time offers (PPOs). At BTW Group, a large share of our full-time hires started as interns. Explore our internship program.

WhatsApp & Telegram Groups

Many cities have job alert groups where companies post openings directly. Join fresher job groups for Thane, Mumbai, and Pune. Always verify the company before sharing personal documents.

3. How to Apply Effectively

Applying is not about quantity — it is about quality. Here is how to make every application count:

  • Customise your resume for each role: Adjust your skills and summary to match the job description. Never send the exact same resume to every company.
  • Match keywords from the job description: Many companies use ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) that filter resumes by keywords. If the job asks for "MS Excel," make sure your resume includes "MS Excel."
  • Write a short cover note: When applying by email, add 3-4 sentences explaining why you are a good fit. Mention the specific role and company.
  • Apply within 48 hours: Jobs get hundreds of applications quickly. Applying early significantly improves your chances of being seen.
  • Track your applications: Keep a simple spreadsheet of where you applied, the date, and the status. Follow up after 5-7 days if you hear nothing.

✓ Do This

  • Use a professional email address (not coolguy99@gmail.com)
  • Save your resume as a PDF with your name in the filename
  • Proofread for spelling and grammar errors
  • Apply to 10-15 well-matched roles per week
  • Follow up politely after 5-7 days

× Avoid This

  • Applying to 50 random jobs in one day
  • Lying about skills or experience
  • Using unprofessional email addresses
  • Sending resumes with formatting that breaks
  • Ignoring instructions in the job post

4. Prepare for the Interview

Getting an interview call means your resume worked. Now you need to convert it into an offer. Preparation is the key:

  • Research the company: Know what the company does, its services, locations, and values. Mentioning something specific about the company in the interview shows genuine interest.
  • Practise common questions: Prepare answers for "Tell me about yourself," "Why should we hire you?" and "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?" Read our guide on answering "Why should we hire you?" with sample answers.
  • Prepare your own questions: Asking 2-3 thoughtful questions (training, growth, team) shows enthusiasm. "What does a typical day look like?" is always safe.
  • Dress professionally: Even for informal interviews, first impressions matter. Wear clean, formal attire.
  • Arrive 10-15 minutes early: Whether in-person or on a video call, punctuality signals professionalism.

5. Follow Up After the Interview

Most freshers stop after the interview and wait for a call. Smart candidates follow up — and it makes a real difference:

  • Send a thank-you message: Within 24 hours, send a short, professional email thanking the interviewer and restating your interest.
  • Ask about next steps: In your follow-up, ask about the timeline for the decision. It shows you are serious.
  • Follow up again after 5-7 days: If you have not heard back, send a polite follow-up. Keep it brief — one paragraph.
  • Keep applying: Never stop your job search until you have a signed offer. Continue applying to other roles while you wait.

6. The 4-Week Job Search Plan

If you are serious about getting a job, treat the search like a full-time job. Here is a simple 4-week plan:

W1
Prepare
Finalise resume, LinkedIn profile, target roles, and list of 20-30 target companies.
W2
Apply Daily
Apply to 10-15 roles per week. Set job alerts. Track every application.
W3
Interview Prep
Practise answers daily, research companies before each interview, do mock interviews.
W4
Convert & Follow Up
Send thank-you notes, follow up on applications, evaluate offers, and negotiate politely.

Job Search Is a Numbers Game

Expect around 20 applications to yield roughly 3-5 interview calls and 1-2 offers. If you are not getting calls, revise your resume and improve your preparation — do not just send more applications with the same resume.

7. Common Fresher Mistakes to Avoid

  • Generic resumes: Sending the same resume everywhere is the #1 reason freshers get rejected.
  • Overambitious applications: Applying for senior roles you are not qualified for wastes time. Start with entry-level and grow.
  • Skipping follow-ups: Many candidates lose opportunities simply because they never followed up.
  • Ignoring soft skills: Communication, punctuality, and attitude matter as much as technical skills for fresher roles.
  • Giving up too early: Most freshers find a job in 4-10 weeks. Consistency beats intensity.

FAQs About Job Search for Freshers

How long does it take a fresher to find a job in 2026?

On average, freshers find a job in 4-10 weeks with consistent, structured effort. Candidates who apply to 10-15 well-matched roles per week, customise their resumes, and follow up tend to land offers much faster than those applying randomly.

Is it better to apply directly on company websites or through job portals?

Both, in combination. Company career pages often post jobs first and receive fewer applications, so your chances are higher there. Job portals help you discover more opportunities. Apply through both channels.

How many jobs should I apply to per week as a fresher?

10-15 well-matched roles per week is a good target. Quality matters more than quantity. Each application should have a resume customised to that role and a short cover note where possible.

Should I accept any job offer as a fresher?

Not any offer, but be realistic. Your first job does not need to be your dream job — it needs to give you experience, learning, and a professional reference. Evaluate salary, growth, training, and work environment before deciding.

Can I get a job in Thane or Mumbai without experience?

Yes. Many companies, including BTW Group, hire freshers without experience and provide full training. Roles like visa executive, documentation executive, sales executive, and customer service are excellent entry points. Check our current openings for fresher-friendly roles.

What should I do if I keep getting rejected?

Do not take rejection personally. Ask for feedback where possible, revise your resume, practise your interview skills, and keep applying. Reviewing our guides on why companies ask for experience and top skills for freshers can also help you understand what employers want.

Are internships a better starting point than full-time jobs?

For students and uncertain freshers, yes. Internships let you build experience, test a role, and often convert to a full-time offer (PPO). For freshers ready to commit, a full-time entry role is equally valid. Read more about why internships matter for career growth.

How do I negotiate my salary as a fresher?

For fresher roles, salary ranges are usually fixed, but you can ask about growth timelines, increments, and benefits. Negotiate politely and professionally — never demand. Focus on demonstrating your value and willingness to learn.
